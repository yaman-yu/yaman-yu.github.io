Human-Centered AI Safety, Usable Security & Privacy, HCI

I study human-centered safety, security, and privacy for emerging technologies, with a focus on generative AI, youth safety, accessibility, and decentralized systems.

Starting in September 2026, I will join the University of Washington Information School as an Assistant Professor. I received my Ph.D. training in the School of Information Sciences at UIUC, advised by Yang Wang, and my M.S. from Carnegie Mellon University, advised by Alessandro Acquisti.

Research

My research bridges human factors with system design and development to create human-centered safety, security, and privacy mechanisms for emerging technologies. I use empirical methods, system building, and design research to understand how risks emerge in real use and how technical systems can better support people.

AI safety for youth and families

Risk taxonomies, safety benchmarks, safeguard models, and design principles for developmentally appropriate AI systems.

Usable security and privacy

Human-centered mechanisms for privacy, security decision-making, and inclusive protection across digital systems.

Accessibility and emerging platforms

AI-enhanced accessibility design, safer online browsing, Web3 social platforms, and cryptocurrency wallet security.

Prospective Students
